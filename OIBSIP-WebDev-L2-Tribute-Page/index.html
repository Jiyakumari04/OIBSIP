A Tribute

Savitribai Phule

3 January 1831 — 10 March 1897

India's first female teacher, who opened a school for girls when doing so could cost her everything — and did it anyway.

A Childhood Marriage, An Unlikely Education

Savitribai was born in 1831 in Naigaon, a village in what is now Maharashtra, and married to Jyotirao Phule at the age of nine — an ordinary arrangement for the time. What made her story extraordinary is what happened next: Jyotirao, himself a reformer, taught her to read and write at home, at a time when education for women, particularly from her caste background, was almost unthinkable.

Savitribai didn't stop at literacy. She trained further in Pune and Ahmednagar to become a qualified teacher, becoming the first woman in India recorded to have done so. What she had gained privately, she immediately set out to give publicly — to girls who had no path to it at all.

Walking to School Under Stones

In 1848, Savitribai and Jyotirao opened a school for girls at Bhide Wada in Pune — widely regarded as India's first. She was both its teacher and its most visible target. On her walk to class each morning, she was jeered at, and accounts describe people throwing stones, mud, and cow dung at her as she passed. She is said to have carried a spare sari to change into once she reached the schoolroom.

She did not stop walking. Over the following years, she and Jyotirao went on to open several more schools across Pune, deliberately including girls from castes that mainstream institutions refused to admit — extending education to communities that upper-caste reformers of the era often left untouched.

    Leads Jyotirao's funeral rites

    After her husband's death, she breaks tradition by lighting his funeral pyre herself and continues his reform work.

  • 1897

    Dies while treating plague patients

    Contracts the plague while running a clinic she opened to care for those affected by the outbreak in Pune, and dies shortly after.

Go, Get Education
Words widely attributed to Savitribai Phule, urging the marginalised toward learning

Why It Still Matters

Savitribai Phule's work did not stay confined to one school or one city. It set a template — that education was a right to be fought for, not a privilege to be granted — that shaped the reform movements which followed her across India for the next century.

India's First Girls' School

The 1848 school at Bhide Wada is widely credited as the first institution in India built specifically to educate girls.

A Published Poet

Her collections "Kavya Phule" and "Bavan Kashi Subodh Ratnakar" are among the earliest known published works by an Indian woman.

Care Beyond the Classroom

She ran shelters for widows and orphans, and died running a clinic for plague patients — reform that extended into public health and welfare.

A National Icon Today

Her birth anniversary, 3 January, is observed as Balika Din (Girl Child Day) in Maharashtra, and Pune University was renamed in her and Jyotirao's honour.
